To the Editor-in-Chief and Associate Editors,
IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security

Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled "PixelRoot: Hardware-Anchored,
Sensor-Level Media Provenance with Real-Time Blockchain Notarization for
Trustworthy Imaging" for consideration as a regular paper in the IEEE
Transactions on Information Forensics and Security.

The proliferation of photorealistic AI-generated imagery has outpaced
detector-based defenses, which remain locked in an adversarial arms race and
generalize poorly to unseen generators. Existing provenance schemes (e.g.,
C2PA) anchor trust in detachable metadata. This paper proposes an alternative:
moving the root of trust into the pixels themselves at the moment of capture,
binding it to a signing key that only the capturing sensor holds, and
anchoring both--the sensor's certification and each capture--on a public
blockchain. We believe this directly addresses core concerns of TIFS readers
in media forensics, watermarking, and content authenticity.

The principal contributions of the work are:

-   a hardware-rooted, dual-binding provenance architecture that combines an
    in-pixel, error-corrected spread-spectrum payload (soft binding) with
    blockchain notarization of a sensor-signed capture record (hard binding),
    organized into explicit assurance tiers;

-   a device-identity layer on the same ledger: on-die sensor keys certified
    by production lot, time-scoped device and manufacturer revocation, and
    capture records bound to a content fingerprint, with a tested Solidity
    registry (measured gas) and a capture-time lower bound from a block
    anchor;

-   a security analysis with five propositions, including identity
    non-transferability, and an empirical demonstration that the in-pixel mark
    alone can be copied without any key (78% of trials) while the
    content-bound record rejects all 240 copy, transplant, and splice
    forgeries on held-out images;

-   an evaluation on public images (the Kodak suite) with production codecs
    (libjpeg, libwebp) and a real Reed-Solomon code under a WAVES-aligned
    attack suite: PSNR ~ 38 dB, payload recovery on 22-23/24 images down to
    JPEG Q20 (21/24 at Q10), zero false accepts over 1224 wrong-key/unmarked
    trials, and tamper localization at ROC-AUC 0.97, with the geometric-attack
    limitations reported; and

-   a manufacturing feasibility analysis of per-pixel gain control and a
    privacy-preserving notarization design (no raw PII on-chain).

I confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere, in whole or in part.
A preprint of this manuscript is posted on arXiv, as IEEE policy permits. The
work is the sole author's. There are no conflicts of interest to disclose, and
the work received no external funding. The reference implementation and the
evaluation harness are released openly so that all reported numbers are
reproducible end-to-end.

I would be grateful for the opportunity to have this work reviewed and am

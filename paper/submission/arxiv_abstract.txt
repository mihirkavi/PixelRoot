AI can now generate photos and videos that people cannot tell from real recordings. Deepfake detectors fail on unseen generators, and provenance standards such as C2PA keep their proof mainly in file metadata, which is lost when a platform strips or re-encodes the file. We present PixelRoot, which keeps proof of origin where it cannot be stripped: in the pixels and on a public blockchain. At capture, the image sensor writes an error-corrected identifier into the image through a faint, keyed change in pixel gain that survives compression. The sensor also signs a capture record with a key that never leaves the chip; the record ties the identifier to the image's hashes and a content fingerprint, and its hash is sealed on the blockchain, where manufacturers also certify and revoke sensor keys. A verifier can thus confirm an exact camera original, confirm that a re-compressed copy derives from a genuine capture, or fail safe to "unverified". On the Kodak images with real codecs, the identifier survives JPEG compression down to quality 20 on 22-23 of 24 images (21 at quality 10) at 38 dB PSNR, with no false accepts in 1,224 trials. An identifier copied onto another image no longer matches the signed fingerprint, so every cross-scene copy and large splice is rejected (0/180), while 90% of processed genuine copies verify. A tested smart contract seals each batch of captures for 47k gas, and we show how sensor makers can build the required gain control.

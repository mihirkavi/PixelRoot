\noindent Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled \textbf{``PixelRoot:
Hardware-Anchored, Sensor-Level Media Provenance with Real-Time Blockchain
Notarization for Trustworthy Imaging''} for consideration as a regular paper in
the \textit{IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security}.

The proliferation of photorealistic AI-generated imagery has outpaced
detector-based defenses, which remain locked in an adversarial arms race and
generalize poorly to unseen generators. Existing provenance schemes (e.g.,
C2PA) anchor trust in detachable metadata. This paper proposes an alternative:
moving the root of trust into the pixels themselves at the moment of capture,
binding it to a signing key that only the capturing sensor holds, and anchoring
both---the sensor's certification and each capture---on a public blockchain. We
believe this directly addresses core concerns of TIFS readers in media
forensics, watermarking, and content authenticity.

The principal contributions of the work are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a hardware-rooted, dual-binding provenance architecture that combines an
        in-pixel, error-corrected spread-spectrum payload (soft binding) with
        blockchain notarization of a sensor-signed capture record (hard
        binding), organized into explicit assurance tiers;
  \item a device-identity layer on the same ledger: on-die sensor keys certified
        by production lot, time-scoped device and manufacturer revocation, and
        capture records bound to a content fingerprint, with a tested Solidity
        registry (measured gas) and a capture-time lower bound from a block
        anchor;
  \item a security analysis with five propositions, including identity
        non-transferability, and an empirical demonstration that the in-pixel
        mark alone can be copied without any key (78\% of trials) while the
        content-bound record rejects every cross-scene copy and large splice on
        held-out images (0/180), together with an adaptive attack that defines
        precisely what a transcoded verdict can and cannot certify;
  \item an evaluation on public images (the Kodak suite) with production codecs
        (\texttt{libjpeg}, \texttt{libwebp}) and a real Reed--Solomon code under a
        WAVES-aligned attack suite: PSNR $\approx$~38~dB, payload recovery on
        22--23/24 images down to JPEG~Q20 (21/24 at Q10), zero false accepts over
        1224 wrong-key/unmarked trials, and tamper localization at
        ROC-AUC~$0.97$, with the geometric-attack limitations reported; and
  \item a manufacturing feasibility analysis of per-pixel gain control and a
        privacy-preserving notarization design (no raw PII on-chain).
\end{itemize}
